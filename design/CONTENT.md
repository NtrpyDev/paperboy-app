## Paper 1 (the main example, use it for the full explainer)

- Title: Observation of Gravitational Waves from a Binary Black Hole Merger
- Authors: B. P. Abbott et al. (LIGO Scientific Collaboration and Virgo Collaboration)
- Journal: Physical Review Letters 116, 061102. Published February 11, 2016. Peer-reviewed. License CC BY 3.0.
- Field: Physics
- Event name: GW150914, recorded September 14, 2015 at 09:50:45 UTC.
- Two detectors: Hanford, Washington and Livingston, Louisiana. Each is an L-shaped laser interferometer with 4 km arms.
- The signal reached Livingston about 7 milliseconds before Hanford.
- The signal swept up in frequency from 35 Hz to 250 Hz in about 0.2 seconds (a "chirp").
- Peak strain: 1.0 x 10^-21. That is a change in arm length far smaller than a proton.
- Source: two black holes of about 36 and 29 solar masses merging into one of about 62 solar masses.
- About 3 solar masses of energy left as gravitational waves.
- Distance: about 410 megaparsecs, roughly 1.3 billion light years.
- Significance: greater than 5.1 sigma. A false alarm this strong is expected less than once in 203,000 years.
- First direct detection of gravitational waves and first observation of a binary black hole merger.
- Honest limits to mention: the sky position was known only to within about 600 square degrees; the mass and distance values carry wide error ranges (for example the distance is 410 with a range of roughly 230 to 570 megaparsecs).

Plain-language hook: "Two black holes merged. Earth felt space stretch."
Card summary: "LIGO's two detectors caught a ripple in spacetime lasting a fifth of a second. Its shape matches black holes of about 36 and 29 solar masses spiraling into one."

## Paper 2 (second feed card)

- Title: Highly accurate protein structure prediction with AlphaFold
- Authors: J. Jumper et al.
- Journal: Nature 596, 583 to 589 (2021). Peer-reviewed. Open access, CC BY 4.0.
- Field: Biology
- Result: in the CASP14 blind test, AlphaFold's median backbone accuracy was 0.96 angstroms (r.m.s.d. at 95% coverage); the next best method scored 2.8 angstroms. For scale, a carbon atom is about 1.4 angstroms wide.

Hook: "A neural network learned to fold proteins."
Summary: "Given only a protein's amino acid sequence, AlphaFold predicted its 3D shape to within about the width of an atom in a blind test."

## Paper 3 (third feed card, a short card style is fine)

- Title: First M87 Event Horizon Telescope Results. I. The Shadow of the Supermassive Black Hole
- Authors: The Event Horizon Telescope Collaboration
- Journal: The Astrophysical Journal Letters 875, L1 (2019). Peer-reviewed. Open access, CC BY 3.0.
- Field: Space
- Result: eight radio telescopes observing together at 1.3 mm wavelength in April 2017 imaged a bright ring 42 microarcseconds across around the black hole in galaxy M87, about 55 million light years away. The black hole's mass: about 6.5 billion Suns.

Hook: "The first picture of a black hole's shadow."
