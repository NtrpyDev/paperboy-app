# Reading a computer science paper as a computer scientist

Settle each of these from the paper itself before writing the brief. Where the paper does not answer one, say so in the brief's limits.

- The task and the claim: faster, more accurate, cheaper, or newly possible? Against which baselines, and are they strong and current?
- Benchmarks used, whether test data could have leaked into training, and whether results hold across more than one dataset.
- Variance: number of runs, error bars, and whether gains exceed the noise.
- Compute and data cost; whether the comparison is at equal cost.
- For theory: the model of computation, the exact bound, and the previous best.
- For systems and security: the threat model or workload, and what was measured on real systems.
- Whether code, data, or weights are released, and what was left out of the evaluation.
