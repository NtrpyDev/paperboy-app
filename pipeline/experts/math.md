# Reading a math paper as a mathematician

Settle each of these from the paper itself before writing the brief. Where the paper does not answer one, say so in the brief's limits.

- State exactly what is proved: the full hypotheses and the conclusion. Proved, conditionally proved, computer-assisted, or conjectured?
- What was known before: the previous best bound or the cases already settled.
- The key new idea, in one paragraph, and which existing tools it combines.
- A small concrete example that shows the statement in action.
- Whether the proof is peer reviewed, formally verified, or a fresh preprint; long proofs of famous problems deserve an explicit caution.
- What the result does not imply, especially popular overreadings.
- Definitions: every term in the statement needs a plain definition and an example.
