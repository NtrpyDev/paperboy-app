# Reading a social science paper as a quantitative social scientist

Settle each of these from the paper itself before writing the brief. Where the paper does not answer one, say so in the brief's limits.

- Design: experiment, natural experiment, survey, or observational data? What supports any causal claim?
- Who the sample is, how they were recruited, and how far the result generalizes.
- Effect sizes in meaningful units, with confidence intervals.
- Preregistration, multiple comparisons, and whether this is a replication.
- How the key concept was measured, and whether that measure is validated.
- Confounders and alternative explanations the authors address or leave open.
- Whether the finding is from one study or a body of work; behavioral effects often shrink on replication.
