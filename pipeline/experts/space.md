# Reading a space paper as an astronomer

Settle each of these from the paper itself before writing the brief. Where the paper does not answer one, say so in the brief's limits.

- What was observed, with which telescope or mission, at which wavelengths, and for how long.
- Distance, redshift, and how they were determined; lookback time when it matters.
- Detection significance and the false alarm rate; how many candidates were examined.
- Selection effects: what the survey could not have seen.
- Which quantities are measured and which are inferred through a model (masses, ages, compositions).
- Alternative interpretations the authors rule out, and those they do not.
- Scale: translate sizes, masses, and distances into comparisons a reader can picture.
